🐍 Porsche vs. Volkswagen
Pac-Man Defense Timeline

From ambition to counter-attack · 2004 – 2012 (Simple English)

📌 Why did Porsche want to buy VW? What role did the VW Law play?
2004 – 2005
🧠 Why Porsche thought about buying VW
• Same roots: Both companies were started by Ferdinand Porsche. A merger could cover cheap cars and luxury sports cars.
• Family fight: Ferdinand Piëch (Piëch family) controlled VW. The Porsche family wanted to take back power.
• Big ambition: VW was huge. If Porsche owned VW, it would get big profits and more control. That's why they started planning a “snake eats elephant” move.
Before 2007
(Old Law)
⛔ The problem: The "VW Law" (Volkswagen Act)
The VW Law said: No shareholder can have more than 20% voting rights (even if you own more shares). The state of Lower Saxony had a special veto over takeovers.
➡️ Meaning: Porsche could buy many shares but still could not control VW. This law was the biggest wall.

⚖️ Abolishing the VW Law (2007) – The key that unlocked everything

🔑 What did it change?
Porsche sued the VW Law in the European Court. In 2007, the court said the VW Law was not allowed under EU rules.
✅ No more 20% voting limit → more shares = more votes.
✅ No more state veto → the government could not block a takeover alone.
🎯 Why was this so important? After 2007, Porsche's shares actually gave power. Without this change, Porsche could never control VW, no matter how many shares they bought. It was the first domino to fall.

📖 Correct name: VW Law or Volkswagen Act (Not "VM Law")
2007
🏛️ European Court kills the VW Law
The court said the VW Law broke free capital movement rules. Voting cap and state veto were removed.
➡️ Now Porsche could use its shares to really control VW. The path was open.
2007 – Oct 2008
🤫 Porsche's secret weapon: Hidden options
Porsche used a loophole in German law: they bought "cash-settled options" – no need to tell the market.
• Public shares: 42.6%
• Hidden options: 31.5%
• Total control: 74.1% (only 0.9% away from 75% target)
Lower Saxony owned 20.1% → only 5.9% of shares were free to trade.
Oct 2008
📈 "The Century Short Squeeze" – €210 → €1,005
Porsche announced its real stake. Many hedge funds had bet against VW (short selling). But only 5.9% of shares were free. They panicked and bought like crazy. VW stock price jumped to €1,005 in two days – VW became the world's most valuable company for a short time.
Short sellers lost tens of billions of euros. Some funds went to zero.
Late 2008 – 2009
💥 Too much debt: Financial crisis hits Porsche
Porsche borrowed over €10 billion to buy VW shares (high leverage). Then the 2008 global financial crisis came: car sales fell, banks stopped lending. Porsche had no cash and was close to bankruptcy.
⚠️ Big turn: The hunter (Porsche) became weak. That opened the door for VW.
2009
🔄 Pac-Man Defense begins: Volkswagen buys Porsche back
Volkswagen saw Porsche's debt crisis and launched the Pac-Man Defense – the target turns around and buys the attacker.
✅ VW bought 49.9% of Porsche's car business for €3.9 billion and became the main shareholder.
🎯 The hunted became the hunter.
2012
🏁 Final result: Who won?
• Volkswagen paid another €4.46 billion and got 100% of Porsche's car brand. Porsche became the 10th brand under VW Group (like Audi, Bentley).
• But as part of the deal, Porsche Holding (Porsche SE) received 50.7% of VW Group's voting shares. That means the Porsche family controls Volkswagen Group.
✨ A strange balance: The brand belongs to VW, but the family controls VW. No clear loser – a "you-in-me" ending.

🔍 Why was abolishing the VW Law so important?

1️⃣ Removed the voting limit
Before 2007, even if Porsche owned 74% of VW shares, they could only vote with 20% power. After the law was gone, more shares = more control.

2️⃣ No more government veto
Lower Saxony lost its special right to block takeovers. The state became a normal shareholder.

3️⃣ Made the whole story possible
Without killing the VW Law, Porsche could never start its secret plan. And later, VW could not freely buy Porsche back. The 2007 court decision was the key that opened the game.

💡 Simple summary: The VW Law was a big wall. Removing it let Porsche enter the ring… but also let Volkswagen fight back with the Pac-Man Defense.
🎓 Key lesson of the Pac-Man Defense: Attack is the best defense – but only if you strike when your enemy is weak.
Porsche won the legal battle and the short squeeze, but too much debt and the financial crisis gave VW the chance to turn the table.
No one is always the hunter. Timing, laws, and money all matter.
📅 Simple timeline: 2004 (ambition) → 2007 (VW Law killed) → 2008 (short squeeze) → 2009 (VW's Pac-Man move) → 2012 (shared control)
